    Your VIN is saved only on this device. It identifies the model, not options or year.

    What it does

    • Identifies the make and W140 model (S320, S420, S500, S600, CL600) from the VIN.
    • Shows only parts that fit your model and year.
    • Puts genuine and aftermarket prices side by side; choose per row.
    • Mix USD, EUR, CNY and JPY prices and still get one USD total.
    • Each row has "Where to buy" links. Clicking a shop link copies the part number, so just paste it into the shop’s search box.
    • Your prices and ticks are saved on this device only. Nobody else can see them.

    What it cannot do

    • No checkout here. Order from each shop.
    • Prices are US dealer list prices, updated on the 30th, and may not be the lowest.
    • The VIN does not reveal options such as ADS or self-leveling, or the year. This is not the Mercedes EPC. US-market cars (starting WDBGA) and the S280, S350, CL420 and CL500 are not identified.
    • 2026 (November 13): 168 part numbers were checked against the US Mercedes dealer catalog (MB Parts Giant), both per part and per model year, and models, years, superseded numbers and prices were corrected. Based on US specs, so other markets may differ.
    • Most part numbers are for the 1996 model year. Rows with a red "check number" tag, or without a year tag, should be checked against your VIN before ordering.
    • Beyond the W140, only the make is identified. Cars before 1981 do not have 17-digit VINs.
    • Shift linkage, valve body and MAP sensor are not listed yet.

    Rebuild guide

    A rebuilt (refurbished) part is a used part taken apart, with worn pieces replaced and reassembled. It is the route when new parts are discontinued or too expensive. Use it with the "Find rebuild" button on each row. Ask whether they accept your part number before sending it.

    Never throw away the old part (the core)
    • The core is the failed old part removed from the car. Rebuilders rebuild from this core.
    • Rebuilding is a one-for-one exchange: you give the old part and get a rebuilt one. Many shops will not do it without a core.
    • If you get the rebuilt part first, a core deposit is charged and refunded when the old part arrives.
    • As more parts are discontinued, cores get rare. Without a core, rebuilding gets harder every year.
    • When using a workshop, say "give me back the removed parts" up front. Otherwise they go to scrap.
    • Photograph the part number, then wrap and store it safely. Broken cores are not accepted.
